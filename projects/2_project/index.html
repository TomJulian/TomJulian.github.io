Genetic and multi-omic discovery

Investigating the biology underlying imaging phenotypes and ophthalmic/systemic disease

I use genetic, metabolomic, and other omics datasets to investigate the biological processes underlying imaging-derived phenotypes and ophthalmic and systemic disease.

A central aim of this work is to move beyond statistical association: identifying potential causal relationships, clarifying disease mechanisms, and finding biological pathways that may support future therapeutic or diagnostic research.

Our genome-wide association studies have explored the genetic determinants of retinal layer thickness(Julian et al., 2025), foveal morphology(Green et al., 2025), and deep learning-derived ophthalmic imaging features(Julian et al., 2026).

I have also used causal inference methods to investigate potentially modifiable disease risk factors, including work examining the relationship between strenuous exercise and motor neuron disease(Julian et al., 2021; O’Brien et al., 2025).
