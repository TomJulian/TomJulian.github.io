Imaging phenotyping

Deriving detailed and reproducible phenotypes from ophthalmic images

Ophthalmic images contain rich information about anatomy, disease, and variation between individuals. I develop and apply computational methods to derive detailed, reproducible phenotypes from these images at scale.

These phenotypes can support studies of disease mechanisms, improve the measurement of clinically important traits, and provide new ways to investigate relationships between the eye and the rest of the body.

This work includes the study of retinal layer thickness, foveal morphology, and deep learning-derived imaging features(Julian et al., 2025; Green et al., 2025; Julian et al., 2026).

Ophthalmic imaging provides complementary views of anatomy and vascular structure, including optical coherence tomography, colour fundus photography, and optical coherence tomography angiography.
